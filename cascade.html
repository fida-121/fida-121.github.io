Final Year Project · 2025 – Present

CASCADE

Collaborative Anticipatory Stacking with Collision-Aware Dynamic Execution: a proactive human-robot collaboration system that anticipates what a person will do next during shared assembly, instead of only reacting to it.

RobotFranka Emika Panda (7-DOF)
FrameworkROS 2
PerceptionRGB-D hand tracking
ModelsLSTM / Transformer
InstitutionUET Lahore
StatusIn progress

The problem

In most human-robot collaboration, the robot waits for the person to finish a step and then responds. That makes shared work slow and stop-start. People, by contrast, coordinate by anticipating each other: you reach for the part your partner is not about to use.

CASCADE explores a robot that does the same. It watches the human's hand, predicts the next action, and chooses a complementary part of the assembly to work on, while keeping the human physically safe throughout.

System overview

The system is built as four connected stages, with recovery behavior for when the assembly deviates from plan.

01PerceiveRGB-D camera and hand-tracking pipeline track the human's hand and the workspace.
02PredictSequence models estimate which action the human is about to perform.
03AllocateThe robot takes a complementary component so both work in parallel.
04Execute safelyCollision-aware motion adapts speed and path based on human proximity.

Human intention prediction

I am building a custom manipulation dataset because the task requires labeled, fine-grained human actions during assembly. The recording plan:

  • Participants: target of 60 to 100, with about 20 trials each.
  • Action classes: reaching, grasping, transporting, placing, retracting and idle.
  • Models under investigation: LSTM and Transformer architectures for predicting the next action from the observed hand trajectory.

The goal is to predict early enough that the robot can commit to a useful action before the human finishes theirs.

Anticipatory task allocation

Once the next human action is predicted, the robot selects a component that does not conflict with it, so the two can work at the same time instead of taking turns. The system also monitors the assembly sequence and recovers automatically when a part is misplaced, rather than stopping the whole task.

Collision-aware execution

Safety is handled in ROS 2 with proximity-based zones around the human. As the person gets closer, the robot's behavior changes in steps:

Safe zoneHuman is far away. Normal execution.
Caution zoneHuman is approaching. Speed is reduced or the trajectory is modified.
Critical zoneHuman is very close. The robot stops.

The diagram illustrates the concept only. Zone distances are tuned during hardware testing.
